## 📜 Historical Context

### The Changeover: Comics → Comix

The shift from "comics" (sterilized, Code-approved) to "comix" (uncensored, "x" marks the spot where the bodies are buried) is more than semantics—it's ideological warfare.

**Zap #1** (Spring 1968):
- **Price**: 25¢ (half of mainstream)
- **Print run**: 3,500 copies
- **Publisher**: Charles Plymell with the Print Mint crew
- **Cover tagline**: "The comic that plugs you in!!"

**Zap #4** (Winter 1968):
- **Featured**: Joe Blow's suburban incest orgy
- **Legal outcome**: *People v. Kirkpatrick* conviction upheld
- **Cultural impact**: Made underground comix the enemy of the state

## 🧠 Academic Notes

### The "Joe Blow" Case Study
The incest fantasy that exposed the hypocrisy of nuclear family values. Crumb's satire used incest to savage conformity, but the graphic execution (rape, incest, dysfunction) triggered the first major obscenity prosecution of underground comix.

### The Feminist Schism
When Trina Robbins objected to women being "humiliated and raped" in strips, the Zap crew called her a "feminazi" with "no sense of humor." This fracture birthed *Wimmen's Comix* (1970) and *It Ain't Me, Babe* — the first all-women comix.

### Legal Milestones
- ***People v. Kirkpatrick* (1970)**: NY Court of Appeals upheld conviction despite expert testimony from Gil Kane
- ***Gay News v. Gotch* (1972)**: California Supreme Court ruled police couldn't seize for selling to undercover buyers
- ***Miller v. California* (1973)**: Community standards doctrine destroyed distribution networks
